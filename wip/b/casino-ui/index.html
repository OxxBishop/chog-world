The casino's controls, rethought

The panel was a form floating over the game. These are three ways to put the controls into the game itself, each drawn here over stills of the real casino at the roulette and the slots, on the laptop and a phone. In all three you bet by tapping the felt with a chip in hand, and your Chog's edges become short, glanceable things that light up when they fire, not a list to read.

What I need from you

Your pick: A, B or C (or parts of each). These are drawn mockups over the real game, so the look is exact but nothing moves yet. Once you pick, I build that one in the game and show it to you running.

Direction A My recommendation

Chips in hand

No panel. A rack of real chips along the bottom: pick one (it lifts) and tap the felt to drop it there. The round SPIN button sits beside the rack, your chips are a gold count, and Undo and Clear are two small round keys. Your Chog's edges are a row of gold badges under its portrait, each with a count of what's left today. One glows when it fires, and tapping a badge shows what it does.

Why I'd pick it

It gets the controls out of the way of the table and the reels, and it's how a casino feels: chips in your hand, a button to press. The badges tell you your edges at a glance, and you see them work.

Risk

Tapping the felt needs a clear marker for where a chip will land, so I'd add a soft glow on the spot under your finger. With six badges a row is about as long as it can get on a phone.

Direction B

The table rail

You're seated at the table. The bottom of the screen is the table's padded leather rail with its brass edge, the Starlite's 1958 made solid. Your chips stand in stacks on the rail and you tap a stack to pick it up. Your total is on a brass plate, and SPIN is a brass bell. Your edges are little folded place cards, the kind on a lounge table, and one opens to tell you what it does.

Feel

The most in keeping with the Starlite Lounge you picked. Warm, physical and a little grand: the stacks shrink as you bet, and SPIN is a bell you press.

Risk

The rail is the biggest of the three and covers the most of the view at the bottom. On a phone the place cards only fit four across, so the rest scroll sideways.

Direction C

Arcade deck

The controls are an arcade cabinet's control deck: a dark metal panel with chunky lit buttons for each chip, a big red SPIN button and an LED counter for your chips. Your edges are power-up cards along the top, and one flips gold when it fires. Same pixel look as the loading screen.

Feel

The most game-like and the loudest. The big buttons are easy to hit on a phone, and the cards make your edges feel like power-ups you've collected.

Risk

An arcade sits a little apart from a 1958 lounge, so the room and the controls would pull two ways. The pixel font is small on a phone for anything longer than a word.
